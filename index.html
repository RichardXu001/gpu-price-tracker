What moved this year

  • RTX 4090 rates fell roughly 39% year over year — the tracked median went from $0.69/hr (Jun 2024 reference point in the tracker's history) to $0.42/hr by Sep 2026 (source: Coloprice snapshot history, retrieved Oct 2026).
  • H100 1-year reserved pricing rose about 40% off its low — $1.70/hr in Oct 2025 to materially higher levels through 2026 (source: GetDeploying index cited by Akash's H100 price tracker, retrieved Sep 2026).
  • Spot/interruptible gaps stayed wide — on the RTX 4090, tracked spot listings were 44–52% below on-demand on the same providers (source: GPUFinder, Oct 2026). See spot vs on-demand.

Reading the numbers

Three pricing tiers exist in the market. Consumer cards (RTX 3090/4090) are listed mostly on peer-to-peer marketplaces by individual hosts. Last-generation datacenter cards (A100) sit in the middle. Current-generation datacenter cards (H100 and newer) are listed by professional clouds. Rates within one card can differ by several times depending on tier, which is why range and median are both shown on the per-card pages.

Method

Figures are collected from public trackers (Coloprice, GPUFinder, gputracker.dev) and from provider-published rates quoted by those trackers or by Akash's H100 price write-up. When a tracker and a provider disagree, both are shown with attribution. Figures are point-in-time: the capture date is listed with every number, and the snapshot above was last reviewed in October 2026.

If you want to see how a live marketplace presents per-host pricing next to these tracker medians, browsing a rental marketplace such as SpareHive is a useful sanity check: listing pages show individual hosts' rates rather than aggregates.
