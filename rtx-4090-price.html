RTX 4090 rental price

The RTX 24GB consumer card is the workhorse of the peer-to-peer GPU rental market. Because listings come from many individual hosts, the spread between the cheapest and most expensive listing is wider than for datacenter cards.

Round-the-clock cost

At the tracked median of $0.42/hr, running one RTX 4090 24/7 for a month (730 hours) works out to roughly $307 (figure published by Coloprice alongside the median, retrieved Oct 2026). Median-level on-demand pricing is almost always the wrong way to buy a month of continuous compute — see reserved and spot below.

Price history

The tracked median fell from $0.69/hr (June 2024 reference) to $0.42/hr (September 2026), about −39% (source: Coloprice snapshot history, retrieved Oct 2026). The floor moved too: the tracked on-demand floor was $0.14/hr in the Oct 2026 reading, up 24% against the prior reading within a $0.11–$0.60 six-month band (source: GPUFinder, Oct 2026).

Why listings differ so much

  • Host type. Individual hosts on marketplaces compete on price; professional clouds price the same card above the marketplace median.
  • Reliability. Cheaper interruptible listings can be preempted; dedicated always-on listings cost more.
  • Bundling. Some listings price CPU RAM, disk and bandwidth generously; others charge for overages, so hourly rate alone is not the whole cost.

Marketplace listing pages show per-host prices directly — for example, SpareHive's 4090 listings show each host's rate and machine specs side by side, which is the raw data trackers aggregate.
