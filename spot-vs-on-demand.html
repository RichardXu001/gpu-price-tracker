What "interruptible" means in practice

  • Marketplace hosts (consumer cards). The dominant model is a bid price: you offer a maximum hourly rate, and the instance is paused — not deleted — when a higher bid arrives or the host reclaims the machine. Vast.ai's own FAQ describes interrupted instances as resumable rather than wiped (source: vast.ai FAQ, retrieved Oct 2026).
  • Professional clouds (datacenter cards). Spot programs reclaim capacity with short notice; AWS EC2 Spot advertises discounts up to ~90% with a two-minute interruption notice (AWS EC2 Spot documentation, Oct 2026).

When spot is a bad trade

  • Checkpointing is awkward for your workload and a pause would lose hours of progress.
  • You are serving live traffic — a paused inference endpoint is an outage.
  • Your job's wall-clock is dominated by data reloading rather than compute, so restarts cost more than the discount saves.

For batch fine-tuning with frequent checkpoints, idle-tolerant rendering-style jobs, or bulk evaluation runs, the 30–50% discount usually outweighs interruption risk.
